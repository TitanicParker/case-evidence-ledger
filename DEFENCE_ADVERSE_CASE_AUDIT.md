# PART I — EXECUTIVE DEFENCE POSITION

The claimant's governing documentary theory is substantially accurate as a description of the surviving record but materially overstates what follows from it. The defence should not deny that the presentation entered Neurology as an evolving combination of gait, feet, tone, movement initiation, dexterity and functional decline; that structural foot disease and neurological abnormality coexisted; that the July 2017 record used the words “Not clear”; that Podiatry thereafter managed the feet; that ordinary dopaminergic treatment was deliberately deferred; or that later records contain medication-state and neurological observations capable of bearing on lower-limb symptoms. Those are documentary facts or strong documentary inferences.

The defence case is that the claimant attempts to transform three propositions about documentation into propositions about clinical fault: unresolved causation becomes alleged premature separation; parallel care becomes alleged etiological foreclosure; and absence of a later synthetic narrative becomes alleged failure to reconsider. None of those transformations follows from the documents alone.

First, genuine structural pathology was independently visible and documented. Marked mallet toes, onychogryphosis, callouses/pressure areas, pes planus and hammertoes were not invented by later clinicians. Podiatry involvement therefore had an affirmative clinical object even if neurological posture, tone or loading also contributed. The record itself repeatedly preserves mixed-mechanism explanations. A dual-pathway model is accordingly at least as consistent with the documents as a theory of erroneous partition.

Second, “Not clear” is not a finding that the services were prohibited from acting until one unified causal account was achieved. It records uncertainty. The lawful defence reading is that uncertainty justified parallel management: Neurology could manage severe Parkinsonism while Podiatry addressed structural disease. The claimant is entitled to say the relationship was not expressly resolved before the operational allocation. The claimant is not entitled, without expert evidence, to say that the allocation required prior etiological resolution or that it was clinically improper to proceed amid uncertainty.

Third, the claimant's “local updating versus global reconstruction” proposition is descriptively strong but normatively incomplete. The documents do show local updating more clearly than a single later document reconstructing the 2017 architecture. But the repository identifies no documentary source establishing a duty to produce such a global reconstruction, no required form for it, and no basis for assuming that clinical reconsideration is invalid unless memorialised as a historical synthesis. Local changes in interpretation and treatment may themselves be evidence of reconsideration rather than evidence that reconsideration did not occur.

Fourth, the prolonged levodopa strategy cannot be adjudicated from the documentary record alone. The record gives an articulated contemporaneous rationale: extraordinarily young onset, concern about dyskinesia and impulse-control effects, and a wish to defer ordinary dopamine replacement while trying other symptomatic approaches. Prof Healy's later comments are not simply contrary: he also recorded that PARK II patients may manage on relatively low replacement doses and can become dyskinetic if over-used. The later introduction of levodopa and apparent benefit may support hindsight questions, but whether treatment should have begun earlier is movement-disorders expert territory.

Fifth, the genetics strand is more favourable to the defence than the claimant's narrative suggests. PARK II/PARK2/PRKN was not missed entirely; it was actively suspected, independently strengthened by Prof Healy, and remained live in 2018. The contemporaneous record also records testing limitations and the view that a firm result might not alter current management. Eventual PRKN confirmation validates the earlier hypothesis; it does not by itself prove that earlier deferral of testing was unreasonable or that every historical foot symptom was genetic/dystonic.

Sixth, later medication-state evidence is capable of reopening questions but does not retrospectively identify one continuous symptom generator. Procyclidine response, a 2020 parkinsonian foot throw, 2021 dystonia/gait reasoning, 2023 OFF-related leg pain/shuffling, and 2025 pain-targeted dopaminergic treatment differ in time, phenotype, source status and specificity. The documents repeatedly allow structural, peripheral, neuropathic, dystonic and OFF-related components to coexist. The claimant's cumulative argument therefore remains evidentially significant but mechanistically underdetermined.

The defence should concede the documentary gap: the represented record does not contain an equally explicit later reconstruction answering, symptom by symptom, what the accumulating evidence did to the 2017 “Not clear” relationship. That remains materially difficult. But the gap proves absence of a document, not absence of reasoning, and still less breach, causation or damage. Missing Podiatry records, multidisciplinary material, native advice, later decision notes and follow-up records make any stronger negative inference unsafe.

**Overall:** the governing theory survives as a narrow documentary proposition, not as a self-proving clinical or legal case. It is descriptively true that an unresolved presentation was operationally separated and that local updating is more visible than global reconstruction. Whether that was clinically wrong, whether a global reconstruction was required, whether earlier dopamine treatment or genetic testing was indicated, and whether any alleged delay caused injury all depend materially on expert evidence and missing records.

### P001 — Integrated neurological entry state

**Defence position:** `ACCEPT BUT NEUTRALISE`

**Best defence answer:** The GP record plainly grouped gait, feet, muscle tension, movement initiation, tone, dexterity and work failure within an evolving neurological referral. That establishes referral context, not common causation. An integrated referral is compatible with several simultaneously active problems.

**Facts supporting defence:** `F0002`, `F0005`, `F0017`, `F0019`, `F0027`, `F0034`, `F0037`, `F0038`.

**Tensions supporting defence:** `T001`, `T004`.

**What claimant is entitled to say:** The patient entered specialist assessment through one evolving referral pathway in which the domains had not yet been cleanly separated.

**What claimant is NOT entitled to say:** That referral integration proves a unitary neurological mechanism or makes later differentiation improper.

**Defence vulnerability:** The pre-admission record makes it difficult to portray the foot problem as wholly detached from the neurological presentation from the outset.

**Evidence needed to improve defence:** Original referral triage reasoning, inpatient clerking, multidisciplinary notes, and contemporaneous Podiatry referral text.

### P002 — Structural foot pathology and neurological abnormality coexisted

**Defence position:** `ACCEPT`

**Best defence answer:** This proposition positively assists the defence. The coexistence of marked structural abnormalities and neurological signs is the documentary foundation for mixed causation and parallel specialty management.

**Facts supporting defence:** `F0005`, `F0150`, `F0153`, `F0161`, `F0162`.

**Defence vulnerability:** Mixed pathology can require active coordination; it does not automatically justify durable conceptual separation.

**Evidence needed to improve defence:** Podiatric examination, biomechanical assessment, imaging, orthotic/treatment records and pain expert opinion.

# PART IV — ATTACK THE SIX-STAGE CASE ARCHITECTURE

## 1. Entry

**Can the defence concede the documentary facts but deny the significance?** Yes. The integrated GP referral is conceded.

**Strongest innocent explanation:** The GP properly referred a complex presentation in undifferentiated form because specialist assessment was needed. Referral integration says nothing decisive about ultimate causal unity.

**Resolution:** Partially resolves the claimant's point.

**What remains unexplained:** Why and how the later relationship between neurological lower-limb features and structural feet was conceptually managed after the hospital assessment.

# PART V — THE “NOT CLEAR” ISSUE

The defence should not minimise the words “Not clear.” They are the contemporaneous record and should be conceded. The better defence is to define what uncertainty can lawfully mean.

**Provisional uncertainty:** Strong defence reading. The clinicians had identified structural and neurological abnormalities but had not yet assigned a single mechanism. A provisional state is expected to evolve and does not invalidate action.

**Mixed-mechanism uncertainty:** Strongest reading. The text itself contemplates complete separateness or peripheral neuropathy while other findings show dystonic-looking gait and severe Parkinsonism. The later record also preserves mixed/peripheral explanations even while recognising OFF-related or dopaminergic components.

**Uncertainty compatible with parallel treatment:** Strong. Visible structural lesions could be treated by Podiatry while Neurology managed Parkinsonism. This is the core answer to the claimant's alleged operational conversion.

**Uncertainty that did not require a single causal answer:** Strong but expert-dependent if translated into clinical standard. Many symptoms can have more than one contributor. The documentary record itself never requires a binary choice.

**Uncertainty that might rationally remain unresolved:** Plausible. If symptom control and function can be managed without a definitive etiological allocation, uncertainty may persist. Whether persistence was clinically acceptable here requires expert evidence.

**Harder question — does the later record document a sufficiently clear resolution or reconstruction?** **No, not in the represented record.** It documents later local classifications and treatments—parkinsonian foot throw, dystonia/altered gait, OFF-related leg pain/shuffling, pain-targeted dopaminergic therapy—but no single later document expressly reconstructs the full 2017 relationship. The defence should concede that documentary fact while denying that it proves absence of reconsideration, breach, or harm.

# PART VI — PODIATRY

The strongest defence of the Podiatry pathway begins with the uncontested structural findings. The record includes marked mallet toes, onychogryphosis, callouses/pressure areas, pes planus and hammertoes. Podiatry therefore had an independent therapeutic object regardless of whether abnormal tone, posture or loading contributed.

Referral could be reasonable even if a neurological contribution remained possible. Parallel care is not logically inconsistent with “Not clear”; indeed, it may be the practical response to uncertainty involving two genuine domains. Podiatry can treat lesions, pressure, footwear, callus and deformity while Neurology addresses tone, bradykinesia and gait.

The phrase “as Parkinson's” should be treated cautiously. It is capable of being a priority or contextual label rather than a causal statement. It prevents the defence from arguing that Podiatry allocation itself proves neurological separateness, but equally it prevents the claimant from arguing that Podiatry accepted a Parkinsonian cause.

Podiatry allocation can therefore be neutral rather than a partition. The defence should characterise the pathway as division of therapeutic labour, not division of etiology, unless the original referral/triage documents show otherwise.

The claimant overreads the operational referral if it treats allocation as an etiological finding. P004 and P005 establish only that allocation occurred while uncertainty remained and that allocation cannot itself prove separateness. They do not establish that the clinicians made an erroneous causal determination.

**What the defence cannot safely claim from Podiatry allocation alone:**

- that the feet were definitively non-neurological;
- that Neurology had resolved the relationship before referral;
- that “as Parkinson's” was meaningless;
- that later neurological foot phenomena were unrelated to the earlier presentation;
- that Podiatry records would support a purely structural theory;
- that parallel allocation was adequately coordinated, because the key pathway records are missing.

# PART VII — LEVODOPA / DOPAMINERGIC DEFERRAL

## 1. Therapeutic timing

The defence has a substantial contemporaneous rationale. The July 2017 records identify unusually young onset and explicit concerns that very young patients may be particularly sensitive to adverse effects, may develop dyskinesia more rapidly, and may be more susceptible to impulse-control disorders/punding. The stated policy was to avoid ordinary levodopa/dopamine agonists “for as long as possible” while using other symptomatic approaches.

Prof Healy's September 2017 opinion does not destroy that rationale. He recorded that PARK II patients tend to manage for long periods on relatively low dopamine replacement doses and may become quite dyskinetic if over-used. That cuts both ways: replacement can be useful, but over-treatment risk was real within the clinicians' own contemporaneous model.

The claimant properly points to the counterweight: the plan acknowledged likely future necessity, possible substantial benefit, and possible later regret. Those statements make reconsideration important but do not identify the correct treatment date.

The later record eventually shows the patient feeling undertreated, return of aches/pains/stiffness, ordinary levodopa introduction in 2021, then dystonia/altered gait reasoning and later OFF-related symptoms. That history requires expert assessment of whether the balance should have shifted earlier.

## 2. Evidential value of treatment response

Therapeutic response is not equivalent to historical causal proof. Improvement after dopaminergic treatment may establish that some contemporary symptoms were dopamine-responsive. It does not prove that every earlier structural lesion or pain episode shared the same mechanism.

The 2025 “profound relief” account is particularly vulnerable to overreading because it is partly retrospective and, importantly, preserves mixed or peripheral mechanisms.

## Can the defence explain deferral without explaining how later evidence would trigger reconsideration?

**Only partially.** The defence can explain why deferral began. It is materially harder to explain a prolonged strategy if the record does not show how worsening function, medication intolerance, dystonia, pain, or patient preference were expected to change the balance. A formal written threshold was not necessarily required, but the missing 2021 decision bridge is important.

## Movement-disorders expert evidence required

- whether the initial deferral strategy was within reasonable practice for the patient's age/presentation;
- comparative risks of early levodopa, dopamine agonists and alternative symptomatic treatment at the relevant time;
- whether Prof Healy's PARK II comments support or undermine the strategy;
- what clinical changes ordinarily justify introduction/escalation;
- whether later response supports an inference that treatment should have started earlier;
- whether any delay caused avoidable injury rather than temporary symptom burden;
- whether an acute levodopa challenge was indicated — **no such requirement should be assumed from the documents alone**.

# PART VIII — GENETICS

The defence should characterise PRKN as a hypothesis recognised early rather than a diagnosis missed until 2026. The July record contemplated recessive/genetic Parkinsonism. Prof Healy then wrote that the patient probably had PARK II and suggested testing PARK II first, with a panel if negative. Galway in 2018 continued to identify PARK2/PRKN as suspected.

The contemporaneous record also contains reasons for caution: parkin testing was described as difficult; testing had only relatively recently become available; the value of genetic diagnosis was discussed in terms of pathology, prognosis and possible levodopa sensitivities; and clinicians stated that confirmation would not particularly change current management.

The defence should also rely, where supported by the native record, on the patient's option to pursue testing and the possibility that later technologies offered broader or more informative testing. Those matters require the actual correspondence before being put too strongly.

Eventual pathogenic PRKN confirmation does **not** make the earlier decision unreasonable by itself. It shows that an earlier hypothesis was ultimately correct. Reasonableness must be assessed on testing availability, utility, limitations, consent, counselling and management consequences at the time.

Hindsight must be kept separate from contemporaneous reasonableness. The claimant can say the branch was real and later confirmed. The claimant cannot say confirmation proves negligent delay or retrospectively assigns every foot symptom to PRKN.

**Expert evidence required:** clinical genetics on testing availability/limitations and utility; movement disorders on management consequences; and, if causation is alleged, expert evidence on what earlier confirmation would probably have changed.

# PART XVII — MOTION-TO-DISMISS VERSION

On the documentary record alone, the claimant has not established clinical negligence. The documents show a complex young-onset Parkinsonian presentation with real structural foot disease, neurological lower-limb abnormalities, open etiological uncertainty and an expressly recorded “Not clear” relationship. They do not establish what reasonable specialist practice required in 2017, whether parallel Podiatry/Neurology management was outside that practice, or whether a reasonable clinician was required to resolve one causal model before treating visible structural disease.

The record does not establish causation. Later Procyclidine, levodopa, Stalevo and Sinemet CR observations may be relevant to lower-limb symptoms, but they do not establish that the same symptom persisted unchanged from 2017 to 2025, that one exclusive mechanism caused structural lesions and pain, or that earlier treatment would probably have avoided a defined injury. Those are expert questions.

The record does not establish improper referral. Podiatry had genuine structural pathology to treat. The fact that referral occurred while etiology was unresolved is not evidence that referral was clinically wrong. Nor does the label “as Parkinson's” prove that Podiatry was etiologically linked or unlinked; it may simply describe clinical context or prioritisation.

The record does not establish improper treatment delay. The clinicians documented a contemporaneous rationale for cautious dopamine replacement in an unusually young patient, including concern about dyskinesia and impulse-control effects. Prof Healy's opinion itself acknowledged dyskinesia risk if dopamine replacement was over-used. Whether the balance should have changed earlier cannot be resolved from chronology and later response alone.

The record does not establish a duty to globally rewrite the historical record. It shows repeated local updating: a later foot phenomenon is called parkinsonian; dystonia and altered gait affect prescribing; leg pain/shuffling are assessed as OFF-related; morning pain becomes a dopaminergic target. The absence of one later synthesis does not prove absence of reconsideration and, without governance or expert evidence, does not establish a documentation breach.

Finally, nothing in the repository establishes dishonesty or concealment. The clinicians recorded uncertainty, differential diagnoses, treatment risks, possible future benefit, genetic hypotheses and post-discharge timing. Later shorthand may be incomplete, but incompleteness is not deceit. A documentary case may justify further inquiry; it does not, without expert evidence and the missing records, prove liability.

# PART XIX — CANDID DEFENCE VERDICT

## CLAIMANT DOCUMENTARY CASE MATERIALLY SURVIVES

The defence can neutralise much of the claimant's proposed clinical and legal significance, but it cannot defeat the core documentary architecture without overreaching. The integrated entry state, “Not clear,” subsequent operational allocation, prolonged treatment strategy, later Parkinsonian/OFF/dystonic lower-limb observations, and absence of an equally explicit global reconstruction are all materially supported by the repository.

What survives, however, is narrower than a negligence case. The documents do not establish that Podiatry was improper, that ordinary dopaminergic treatment should have begun at a specific earlier date, that genetic testing was unreasonably delayed, that the later symptoms were identical to the 2017 symptoms, that a global reconstruction document was required, or that any alleged delay caused injury. Those propositions depend heavily on expert evidence and missing records.

The defence therefore has a viable and substantial answer on breach and causation, but not by denying the claimant's documentary sequence. The safe strategy is to concede the sequence, insist on mixed pathology and symptom non-identity, attack the leap from documentary gap to clinical fault, and demand expert proof for every normative or causal step.
