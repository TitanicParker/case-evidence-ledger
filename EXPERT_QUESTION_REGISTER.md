# Case Evidence Ledger — Expert Question Register

**Status:** Controlled expert-question layer  
**Primary basis:** `EVIDENTIAL_TENSIONS.md`, `PROPOSITION_REGISTER.md`  
**Adverse-case basis:** `DEFENCE_ADVERSE_CASE_AUDIT.md`, `ADVERSE_CASE_ADJUDICATION.md`

> **Control rule:** This register identifies questions that require independent expert judgment before stronger clinical, causal or legal propositions can safely be advanced. It does not answer those questions, does not substitute for expert opinion, and does not convert documentary relevance into proof of mechanism, breach or harm.

## Governing function

The documentary record can establish what was recorded, what remained unresolved, what later changed, and where explicit reconstruction is or is not represented. The expert layer begins where clinical significance must be assigned to those documentary facts.

The intended chain is:

**Source → Fact → Tension → Proposition → Expert Question → Expert Opinion → Counsel consequence**

The first five layers belong to the controlled repository. Expert opinion must remain external and independent. Counsel consequence is conditional upon the expert answer and other legal evidence.

## Outcome discipline

Not every expert question is binary. Each question below identifies a bounded outcome range. The purpose is to make the consequences of different legitimate expert answers visible in advance and to avoid drafting the case around a preferred medical conclusion.

# EQ001 — Clinical character of the 2017 combined presentation

**Question:**  
How should an independent movement-disorders expert clinically characterise the July 2017 coexistence of severe Parkinsonism, genuine structural foot pathology, high tone and a footdrop-like gait described as somewhat suggestive of dystonic posture, where the relationship between the leg/foot domains was recorded as “Not clear”?  

**Documentary triggers:** `T001`, `T002`, `T004`  
**Controlled propositions:** `P001`, `P002`, `P003`  

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- the patient entered Neurology through one evolving referral pathway involving feet, gait, tone, movement initiation, dexterity and function;
- genuine structural foot pathology was present;
- neurological lower-limb abnormalities were present;
- the relationship was expressly unresolved in the represented 2017 record.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- whether the gait/posture findings were clinically consistent with dystonia;
- whether structural and neurological mechanisms likely coexisted;
- whether any component can be allocated retrospectively with reasonable confidence.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. Predominantly mixed structural and neurological phenotype.  
B. Predominantly neurological lower-limb phenotype with independent structural disease.  
C. Predominantly structural/peripheral foot pathology with separate Parkinsonian motor findings.  
D. The surviving material is insufficient for reliable allocation.

**Boundary:**  
No outcome may be treated as proving that every historical foot symptom had one mechanism.

**Counsel consequence:**  
The answer defines the permissible clinical starting point for every later causation and reconsideration argument. Outcome A or B materially strengthens the relevance of later lower-limb treatment-state evidence; outcome C narrows that relevance; outcome D preserves the documentary uncertainty without resolving mechanism.

# EQ002 — Clinical significance of the Neurology/Podiatry operational allocation

**Question:**  
Was the 2017 parallel allocation of Parkinsonism to Neurology and the foot problem to Podiatry clinically reasonable in the presence of an unresolved relationship, and what, if anything, should that allocation have implied about ongoing causal reconsideration or coordination?

**Documentary triggers:** `T001`, `T002`, `T003`, `T004`  
**Controlled propositions:** `P003`, `P004`, `P005`  

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- operational allocation occurred while the represented causal relationship remained unresolved;
- Podiatry had a genuine structural clinical object;
- the 6 July GP record used the phrase “as Parkinson's” in relation to Podiatry prioritisation;
- Podiatry allocation alone does not establish etiological separateness.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- whether parallel specialty management was clinically appropriate;
- whether coordination or later re-integration would ordinarily be expected in a mixed phenotype;
- whether the operational split risked becoming an over-stable explanatory partition.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. Parallel management was reasonable and required no special later synthesis beyond ordinary follow-up.  
B. Parallel management was reasonable initially but should have remained explicitly provisional and coordinated.  
C. The allocation materially risked obscuring a neurological component and required active later reconsideration.  
D. The record is insufficient to assess the adequacy of the allocation without missing Podiatry/referral material.

**Boundary:**  
Podiatry involvement must not be characterised as inherently improper.

**Counsel consequence:**  
Only outcomes B or C materially support an argument that later evidence should have triggered re-integration of the original case architecture. Outcome A substantially neutralises that limb. Outcome D makes missing source material central.

# EQ003 — Reasonableness and reopening threshold of dopaminergic deferral

**Question:**  
Given the contemporaneous rationale for deferring levodopa/dopamine-agonist treatment in a very young patient, together with the recorded prospect of substantial benefit, future necessity and possible later regret, was the strategy clinically reasonable over time and what findings should reasonably have triggered its reconsideration?

**Documentary triggers:** `T006`, `T007`  
**Controlled propositions:** `P009`, `P010`, `P011`  

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- deferral was deliberate and reasoned;
- anticipated long-term treatment risks were recorded;
- possible substantial benefit and later necessity were also recorded;
- no single explicit reopening rule is represented in the present record;
- levodopa was eventually introduced in 2021.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- whether the initial strategy met an appropriate movement-disorders standard;
- whether the duration of deferral remained reasonable;
- what symptom, functional or treatment-state developments should have altered the balance;
- whether absence of a formal threshold is clinically material.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. Strategy and duration were clinically reasonable; no earlier trigger is identifiable.  
B. Initial deferral was reasonable, but later evidence should have prompted earlier reconsideration.  
C. The strategy was clinically over-prolonged from an earlier point.  
D. The record is insufficient without missing decision notes and fuller treatment history.

**Boundary:**  
The repository does not assert that an acute levodopa challenge was guideline-required or that levodopa should have been started in July 2017.

**Counsel consequence:**  
Outcome A substantially defeats a treatment-delay limb. Outcome B supports a later missed-reconsideration argument. Outcome C may support a stronger standard-of-care case, subject to full expert reasoning and causation evidence. Outcome D makes disclosure and missing records central.

# EQ004 — Meaning of Procyclidine response and withdrawal evidence

**Question:**  
What clinical significance, if any, should be attributed to the recorded improvement on Procyclidine and deterioration during withdrawal, including the rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon together with worsening tension, gait or restlessness?

**Documentary triggers:** `T009`, `T010`, `T022`  
**Controlled propositions:** `P016`, `P017`, `P023`  

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- Procyclidine was clinically material to the recorded motor state;
- withdrawal generated lower-limb/foot-specific information;
- the episode temporally linked medication state with gait/tension and a foot phenomenon.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- whether the response supports a dystonic or other central motor component;
- whether altered tone, loading, restlessness or pain perception could explain the observations;
- how much weight the episode should carry in retrospectively assessing the 2017 uncertainty.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. Strong evidence of a clinically meaningful central/dystonic component.  
B. Evidence of a central motor influence, but not enough to identify the primary symptom generator.  
C. Nonspecific treatment-state information compatible with several mechanisms and of limited retrospective value.  
D. Insufficiently documented for reliable interpretation.

**Boundary:**  
Medication response does not establish exclusive causation and does not prove that a corn or structural lesion was neurologically caused.

**Counsel consequence:**  
Outcomes A or B materially strengthen the argument that later evidence bore on the original unresolved relationship. Outcome C leaves only modest relevance. Outcome D prevents strong reliance on this strand.

# EQ005 — Clinical continuity of later lower-limb phenomena

**Question:**  
To what extent, if any, are the 2020 parkinsonian foot-throw finding, the 2021 dystonia/altered-gait reasoning, the 2023 OFF-related leg pain/shuffling assessment and the 2025 pain-targeted dopaminergic response clinically continuous with, or informative about, the unresolved lower-limb/foot presentation documented in 2017?

**Documentary triggers:** `T011`, `T012`, `T013`, `T014`, `T015`, `T016`, `T022`  
**Controlled propositions:** `P018`, `P019`, `P020`, `P021`, `P022`, `P023`  

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- later clinicians assigned Parkinsonian or OFF-state significance to some lower-limb phenomena;
- levodopa exposure later increased in the context of dystonia/altered gait;
- dopaminergic treatment was later used in part against morning pain;
- repeated medication-state observations are capable of bearing on the earlier uncertainty.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- symptom identity or non-identity across years;
- whether the later phenomena represent evolution of an earlier dystonic/Parkinsonian component;
- whether mixed structural, neuropathic and OFF-related mechanisms better explain the longitudinal record.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. Substantial clinical continuity with a neurological lower-limb component present from 2017.  
B. Partial continuity: some later phenomena illuminate an earlier neurological component, while other foot pathology remained distinct.  
C. Limited continuity: the later phenomena are clinically different enough that retrospective inference should be weak.  
D. Insufficient data for reliable longitudinal identity analysis.

**Boundary:**  
Similarity across time is not identity. The register does not assume that the 2017, 2020, 2023 and 2025 phenomena were the same symptom.

**Counsel consequence:**  
Outcome A gives P023 substantial clinical force. Outcome B supports a mixed-mechanism reconsideration argument. Outcome C materially narrows the cumulative case. Outcome D requires caution and possibly further expert/source work.

# EQ006 — Clinical significance of the PARK2/PRKN branch and later confirmation

**Question:**  
What should an independent expert infer from the fact that PARK2/PRKN was a specific specialist-supported hypothesis in 2017–2018 and was later confirmed by two pathogenic PRKN variants, with Parkinsonism and dystonia attributed to those mutations?

**Documentary triggers:** `T008`, `T017`  
**Controlled propositions:** `P012`, `P014`, `P015`, `P025`  

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- Prof Healy considered PARK II probable;
- Galway later recorded PARK2/PRKN as a specific suspected cause;
- testing was deferred in part because immediate management impact was considered limited;
- pathogenic PRKN variants were later confirmed and Parkinsonism/dystonia attributed to them.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- clinical utility of earlier testing at the relevant times;
- whether earlier PRKN confirmation should have altered interpretation, counselling, treatment or symptom attribution;
- what the result retrospectively adds to the significance of early dystonic findings.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. Earlier confirmation would likely have had material clinical or interpretive consequences.  
B. It would have refined aetiology but probably not materially altered immediate management.  
C. Its principal value is retrospective confirmation of an already live branch, with little bearing on earlier management.  
D. The record is insufficient to assess earlier utility without genetics-era technical context.

**Boundary:**  
PRKN confirmation validates the historical genetic branch; it does not establish that every historical foot symptom was caused by PRKN or dystonia.

**Counsel consequence:**  
Outcome A may support an earlier-testing or earlier-reinterpretation argument. Outcome B supports a narrower historical-completeness argument. Outcome C largely confines the genetics strand to correcting etiological representation. Outcome D requires specialist genetics evidence before stronger use.

# EQ007 — Adequacy of local updating versus need for broader reconstruction

**Question:**  
Taken cumulatively, did the later medication-state, phenomenological and genetic evidence clinically require, reasonably call for, or not require an explicit broader reconsideration of the original 2017 “Not clear” foot–neurology relationship beyond the local treatment and symptom updates that are documented?

**Documentary triggers:** `T011`, `T014`, `T016`, `T017`, `T022`  
**Controlled propositions:** `P023`, `P024`, `P025`  
**Adverse-case focus:** `D009`, `D010`

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- later evidence repeatedly became capable of bearing on the original unresolved relationship;
- local treatment and symptom interpretation clearly changed over time;
- the represented record does not show an equally explicit later reconstruction of the original relationship;
- absence of one synthesis document does not prove absence of reconsideration;
- local updating may itself evidence reconsideration.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- whether local updating was clinically sufficient;
- whether a broader longitudinal synthesis would ordinarily be expected or clinically material;
- whether failure to articulate such a synthesis, if established, would matter to diagnosis, treatment or risk management.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. The later evidence materially required broader reconsideration and the documented local updates were insufficient.  
B. The later evidence reasonably called for broader reconsideration, but adequacy depends on missing records or context.  
C. Local updating was clinically sufficient; no distinct global reconstruction was required.  
D. The question cannot be answered from the surviving represented record.

**Boundary:**  
P024 is a documentary proposition only. It must not be converted into “no reconsideration occurred”, “a global rewrite was legally required”, or “absence of synthesis caused harm”.

**Counsel consequence:**  
This is the central expert hinge. Outcome A gives P024 substantive clinical significance. Outcome B supports a narrower missed-opportunity/documentation case. Outcome C substantially neutralises the central clinical argument while leaving the documentary gap intact. Outcome D shifts emphasis to disclosure, missing records and evidential uncertainty.

# EQ008 — Causation and avoidable harm, conditional on the preceding answers

**Question:**  
If the expert concludes that clinically material reconsideration or treatment change should have occurred earlier, did any resulting delay probably cause or prolong avoidable pain, disability, treatment burden or other injury, and over what period can that conclusion be supported?

**Documentary triggers:** downstream of `T006`–`T017`, `T022`  
**Controlled propositions:** downstream of `P009`–`P025`  

**What the documents establish regardless:**  
- symptoms, functional impairment, medication changes and later reported treatment responses are documented;
- the repository does not itself establish that any delay caused a particular injury.

**Expert-dependent issues:**  
- counterfactual treatment timing;
- probability and magnitude of symptom improvement under an earlier alternative course;
- whether structural or mixed mechanisms would have limited benefit;
- duration and attribution of any avoidable harm.

**Permitted outcome range:**  
A. Probable material avoidable harm over a clinically supportable period.  
B. Some probable avoidable harm, but narrower than the full historical symptom period.  
C. Earlier reconsideration may have been indicated, but causation of additional harm cannot be established.  
D. No probable avoidable harm attributable to the alleged delay.  
E. Insufficient evidence for a reliable causation opinion.

**Boundary:**  
This question must be asked last. No causation argument should be back-projected into the earlier documentary layers.

**Counsel consequence:**  
Only outcomes A or B support a positive damages/causation limb. Outcome C preserves a possible process or standard-of-care issue without proven injury. Outcomes D or E materially defeat or prevent a compensatory causation case.

# Cross-register controls

The following controls apply to every expert question:

1. **Operational allocation is not synonymous with etiological foreclosure.**
2. **Podiatry proves neither neurological separateness nor neurological causation.**
3. **“Not clear” records unresolved relationship, not a prohibition on parallel care.**
4. **Medication response may justify reconsideration without proving exclusive mechanism.**
5. **Similarity across time does not establish identity of symptoms.**
6. **PRKN confirmation validates the earlier genetic branch, not every retrospective symptom attribution.**
7. **Absence of one global reconstruction document does not prove absence of reconsideration.**
8. **Local updating may itself evidence reconsideration; its adequacy is expert-dependent.**
9. **Documentary propositions do not establish negligence, causation or harm without the appropriate expert bridge.**
10. **Missing source material must remain visible where an outcome depends upon it.**
